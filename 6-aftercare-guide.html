After Your Treatment

Breast Filler Aftercare & Recovery

A calm, complete guide to your recovery — what's normal, what to avoid, and the warning signs to watch for.

Some swelling and tenderness in the first days is completely normal. Your result settles over the following 2–6 weeks as swelling reduces and the filler integrates.

The first few days are not your final result. Please be patient — and never hesitate to reach out to us if anything worries you.

What is completely normal?

Normal & expected

  • Swelling and a feeling of fullness
  • Bruising
  • Tenderness & soreness
  • Firmness, or feeling the filler under the skin
  • Mild redness at injection points
  • Temporary shape change or slight unevenness while swelling settles
  • Discomfort lying on your front for a week or two
Healing is not linear. Some days may feel firmer or more swollen than others — this does not mean anything is wrong.

Immediately after & first 24–48 hours

Please do

  • Keep the area clean and dry
  • Wear a soft, supportive, non-underwire bra for comfort (day and night early on, if advised)
  • Sleep on your back with the chest supported
  • Drink plenty of water and rest
  • Take paracetamol if you need pain relief

Please avoid

  • Touching, pressing or rubbing the area unnecessarily
  • Makeup, creams or fake tan over injection points for 24–48 hours
  • Alcohol for 24–48 hours
  • Smoking / vaping where possible
  • Strenuous exercise, heat and tight underwire

Hygiene

Keeping the area clean

  • Gently cleanse with clean hands; pat dry — don't scrub
  • Don't pick any tiny scabs at injection points
  • Avoid swimming pools, hot tubs and shared water for 1–2 weeks (infection risk)

To massage — or NOT?

Do not massage or manipulate the area yourself unless your practitioner specifically tells you to. With breast filler, pressing or "moulding" the area can move the filler or cause problems. Follow the exact instructions your practitioner gives you at your appointment — they may differ depending on the product used and how you healed.

Supportive bra guidance

For the first 1–2 weeks (unless advised otherwise)

  • Wear a soft, supportive, non-underwire bra for gentle support and comfort
  • Avoid tight or underwired bras that press into a tender, swollen area
  • A soft bra overnight can feel more comfortable early on

Your practitioner will confirm exactly what suits you.

What to avoid while you heal

For 1–2 weeks

  • Strenuous chest / upper-body exercise (chest press, push-ups, heavy lifting)
  • Running & high-impact exercise that bounces the chest
  • Saunas, steam rooms & very hot baths
  • Swimming
  • Sunbeds & excessive heat/sun on the area

Also avoid

  • Sleeping on your front
  • Tight underwire bras
  • Alcohol for 24–48 hours
  • Ibuprofen, aspirin & anti-inflammatories for pain (unless prescribed) — use paracetamol instead

Sleeping position

For the first 1–2 weeks

  • Sleep on your back
  • Avoid sleeping face-down or pressing the chest into the mattress
  • A soft supportive bra overnight can help

Pain relief

You may use

  • Paracetamol, if suitable for you

Avoid (unless prescribed)

  • Ibuprofen
  • Aspirin
  • Anti-inflammatories

These can increase bruising. If your pain is severe or increasing rather than improving, that is a warning sign — see below.

Your review

A review is usually recommended a few weeks after treatment to check how the area has settled and to discuss whether a top-up session is needed to build the volume you'd like. Please attend your review, and keep in touch with any questions in the meantime.

Warning signs — contact the clinic

Contact Layali Clinic if you notice

  • Swelling or pain that is getting worse after a few days rather than better
  • New or growing lumps, hard areas or nodules
  • Increasing redness or warmth over the area
  • Any discharge or fluid from an injection point
  • A raised temperature or feeling generally unwell
  • The area becoming more uneven or shifting noticeably
  • Any symptom that worries you

Emergency signs — act immediately

These are medical emergencies

Vascular occlusion (filler affecting a blood vessel) — call the clinic immediately, and if severe call 999 / attend A&E:

  • Severe or increasing pain out of proportion to what's expected
  • Colour change — skin going white (blanching), or grey, blue, purple or mottled
  • Skin becoming cold, dusky or blistering

Serious infection — call the clinic immediately, and if severe call 999 / attend A&E:

  • Spreading redness and heat over the chest
  • Pus or discharge
  • Fever or feeling very unwell

Also seek emergency help for any difficulty breathing, chest pain, facial/throat swelling or feeling faint (possible severe allergic reaction).
