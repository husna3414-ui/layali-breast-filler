Filler-quantity expectations

Non-surgical breast enhancement with hyaluronic acid (HA) filler is a subtle volumising and contouring treatment. Because the chest is a large area, it typically requires large quantities of product and more than one session to build a safe, natural result gradually. The amount needed is highly individual.

Please understand

  • This is NOT a surgical implant and does not create an implant-sized increase.
  • Results are subtle and temporary. The visible cosmetic result is expected to reduce gradually, but the rate of breakdown is unpredictable, and complete disappearance by a particular date cannot be guaranteed.
  • Multiple sessions are usually needed; a top-up is common a few weeks after the first session.
  • Full results settle over roughly 2–6 weeks as swelling reduces.
  • Results vary between clients and are not guaranteed.

Future pregnancy, breastfeeding & health declaration

Even if you are not currently pregnant or breastfeeding, please understand the following about future pregnancy and breastfeeding:

  • The long-term effects on future breastfeeding may not be established for this product.
  • The natural breast changes of pregnancy may alter your result.
  • Pregnancy and lactation can make filler-related lumps or swelling harder to assess.
  • Any breast symptom that appears during pregnancy or breastfeeding still requires medical assessment — do not assume it is caused by filler.
  • You must disclose your filler history to your maternity and breast-health teams.

Breast implants

Existing implants change the risks and management of breast filler, so we ask about them separately.
Please choose one.
If you have implants, please understand that an infection involving filler near an implant could affect the implant itself and require specialist or surgical management. Capsular contracture (a tightening, hardening capsule around an implant) has been reported in people with implants. We may not be able to treat you until your implant surgeon has been consulted.

Breast filler & future breast imaging

Breast filler is not a substitute for breast screening or for having any lump or change checked by your GP. It is important that you understand injected HA filler in the chest may:

  • appear as collections, masses or cyst-like areas on imaging;
  • obscure normal breast tissue;
  • mimic breast abnormalities;
  • reduce the diagnostic quality of mammography;
  • lead to additional ultrasound, MRI, biopsy or specialist review;
  • remain visible after the cosmetic result appears to have faded;
  • complicate future breast surgery.

What the treatment involves

Non-surgical breast filler involves injecting hyaluronic acid (HA) dermal filler into the tissue of the chest to add subtle volume and define the cleavage. The area is cleansed using an aseptic technique, a local anaesthetic may be used for comfort, and the filler is placed using a needle or cannula.

During and after treatment you may feel pressure, stinging, warmth, mild discomfort and, afterwards, swelling, tenderness and firmness. Because the chest is a large area, large volumes and more than one session are usually needed to build the result safely.

This is an elective, non-surgical treatment. It is not a surgical implant, and it does not produce the result of surgical breast augmentation.

Intended benefits

What this treatment may improve: subtle added volume to the breast/chest, softer and fuller cleavage definition, improved upper-pole fullness, and minor improvement of asymmetry or contour irregularity. The aim is a natural, understated enhancement.

What this treatment cannot do: it is not a surgical implant and does not create an implant-sized increase. It is not a surgical uplift and will not correct significant sagging (ptosis) or markedly reposition the breast or nipple.

What to expect

  • Level of change: subtle — an enhancement rather than a transformation.
  • When results appear: some volume is visible immediately; the final result settles over roughly 2–6 weeks as swelling reduces.
  • When results are assessed: at your review, once swelling has settled; a top-up is commonly needed a few weeks after the first session.
  • How long results last: the visible cosmetic result is expected to reduce gradually, but the rate of breakdown is unpredictable, and complete disappearance by a particular date cannot be guaranteed. See "Longevity & breakdown" below.
  • Maintenance: more than one session and ongoing maintenance top-ups are usually needed to build and preserve the result.
  • No guarantees: results vary between individuals and cannot be guaranteed.

Alternatives to this treatment

You are under no obligation to proceed. Reasonable alternatives include:

  • No treatment — accepting the natural appearance of your breasts.
  • Delaying treatment — taking more time to decide, or waiting until any health, breast-screening or life circumstances are resolved.
  • Sculptra or other collagen-stimulating injectables — a different non-surgical option, where appropriate.
  • Surgical breast augmentation (implants) — carried out by a surgeon, not at this clinic.
  • Surgical uplift (mastopexy) — for sagging, carried out by a surgeon, not at this clinic.
  • Fat transfer — surgical relocation of your own fat, carried out by a surgeon, not at this clinic.
  • Referral to a specialist (plastic/breast surgeon) for surgical options or for any breast-health concern.
  • A second opinion — you are welcome to seek one before deciding.

Risks & complications

No procedure is entirely risk-free. Because breast filler uses large volumes injected into deeper tissue of the chest, it carries a higher risk profile than small facial filler. Risks are grouped below by how commonly they occur. Unforeseen risks may also occur that are not listed here.

Common

  • Pain, stinging or discomfort during injection
  • Bruising, redness, tenderness
  • Swelling (which can be significant with large volumes)
  • Firmness or feeling the filler
  • Temporary shape change while swelling settles
  • Feeling faint during the procedure
  • Subtle or uneven result needing a further session

Less common

  • Lumps, nodules or hard areas
  • Contour irregularity or asymmetry
  • Filler migration or shifting with movement/pressure
  • Prolonged swelling, sensitivity or pain
  • Persistent firmness
  • Delayed inflammatory reaction (weeks–months later)
  • Interference with breast imaging / mammograms

Rare but serious

  • Vascular occlusion — filler blocking a blood vessel, which can cause tissue death (necrosis)
  • Infection, abscess or biofilm in the breast tissue — higher with large volumes and deep tissue; may need antibiotics, drainage or dissolving; can appear weeks or months later
  • Atypical or difficult-to-treat infection, and recurrent infection after apparently successful treatment
  • Breast cellulitis; mastitis-like inflammation
  • Haematoma or internal bleeding; seroma or fluid collection; cyst-like filler collections
  • Granuloma or nodules that may require dissolving or, rarely, surgical management, and could be persistent
  • Capsule or pseudocapsule formation; persistent breast hardening
  • Skin ulceration or wound breakdown; chronic draining wound; loss of skin or breast tissue
  • Filler embolism — filler entering a blood vessel and travelling elsewhere; very rare but can be serious
  • Filler migration into the chest, abdominal wall, axilla or other tissues
  • Enlarged or reactive lymph nodes
  • Chronic pain; altered breast or nipple sensation; changes in nipple position
  • Worsening ptosis / loose skin as filler reduces; noticeable differences between breasts as filler breaks down unevenly
  • Need for aspiration, drainage or biopsy; need for surgical removal
  • Permanent breast deformity; permanent scarring after treating a complication
  • Difficulty having future implants, uplift, reduction or other breast surgery
  • Inability to remove every trace of filler; recurrence after dissolving or drainage
  • Damage to underlying structures (blood vessels, nerves, soft tissue)
  • Skin changes — pigment change, prolonged redness, scarring (including keloid)
  • Immediate hypersensitivity reaction, including, very rarely, anaphylaxis
Extremely rare — systemic embolic complications. Filler entering the bloodstream has, very rarely, been linked to pulmonary and ocular complications: sudden visual disturbance or visual loss, severe breathlessness, chest pain, or neurological symptoms. Seek emergency help immediately if these occur.
Because some injections lie near the chest wall, there is a rare possibility of pleural injury and pneumothorax (air around the lung).

Hyaluronidase is not a simple rescue. Hyaluronidase (which dissolves HA filler) may be considered for some complications, but it does not guarantee complete removal or correction. Large-volume filler, infection, abscess, tissue damage or migrated material may require repeated treatment, aspiration, drainage, specialist referral or surgery. Hyaluronidase carries its own risks, including allergic reaction and rare anaphylaxis, and should not be used at sites of infection or malignancy.

Longevity & breakdown

HA breast filler is naturally broken down by the body over time. The visible cosmetic result is expected to reduce gradually, but the rate of breakdown is unpredictable. Residual filler may remain detectable on examination or imaging beyond the expected cosmetic duration, and complete disappearance by a particular date cannot be guaranteed. Maintenance top-ups are usually needed to preserve the result.

Emergency warning signs — act quickly

Contact Layali Clinic immediately on 07787 708200 if you develop:

  • Severe or rapidly increasing pain
  • White, grey, blue, purple or black skin
  • Blistering or skin breakdown
  • Rapidly increasing swelling
  • Spreading redness or heat
  • Pus or discharge
  • Fever or feeling severely unwell

Call 999 or go to A&E immediately if you develop:

  • Breathing or swallowing difficulty
  • Facial or throat swelling
  • Chest pain
  • Visual disturbance or loss of vision
  • Weakness, confusion, collapse or other neurological symptoms

Acting quickly matters — some complications are time-critical, so please do not wait. Out of hours, if you cannot reach the clinic, call NHS 111, or 999/attend A&E for anything severe or rapidly worsening.

Tell any other healthcare professional (A&E, GP, 111) the treatment you had, the product, the date and the injection/treatment site(s), and show them this consent so they can help you quickly.
