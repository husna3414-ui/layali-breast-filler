What the treatment involves

Non-surgical breast filler involves injecting hyaluronic acid (HA) dermal filler into the tissue of the chest to add subtle volume and define the cleavage. The area is cleansed using an aseptic technique, a local anaesthetic may be used for comfort, and the filler is placed using a needle or cannula.

During and after treatment you may feel pressure, stinging, warmth, mild discomfort and, afterwards, swelling, tenderness and firmness. Because the chest is a large area, large volumes and more than one session are usually needed to build the result safely.

This is an elective, non-surgical treatment. It is not a surgical implant, and it does not produce the result of surgical breast augmentation.

11

Benefits, results & alternatives

Possible benefits include subtle added volume, softer cleavage definition, minor asymmetry improvement, no scars and minimal downtime compared with surgery. Results are subtle, temporary (typically ~12–18 months) and vary between individuals; more than one session and ongoing maintenance are usually needed.

Alternatives include: no treatment, surgical breast augmentation (implants), and fat transfer — both surgical options carried out by a surgeon, not at this clinic. I understand I have the right to decline treatment or choose an alternative.

12

Risks & complications

No procedure is entirely risk-free. Because breast filler uses large volumes injected into deeper tissue of the chest, it carries a higher risk profile than small facial filler. The following risks may occur, and there may be unforeseen risks not listed:

Common / temporary

  • Pain, stinging or discomfort during injection
  • Bruising, redness, tenderness
  • Swelling (which can be significant with large volumes)
  • Firmness or feeling the filler
  • Temporary shape change while swelling settles
  • Feeling faint during the procedure
  • Subtle or uneven result needing a further session

Uncommon

  • Lumps, nodules or hard areas
  • Contour irregularity or asymmetry
  • Filler migration or shifting with movement/pressure
  • Prolonged swelling, sensitivity or pain
  • Persistent firmness
  • Delayed inflammatory reaction (weeks–months later)
  • Interference with breast imaging / mammograms

Rare but potentially serious

  • Vascular occlusion — filler blocking a blood vessel, which can cause tissue death (necrosis)
  • Infection, abscess or biofilm in the breast tissue — higher with large volumes and deep tissue; may need antibiotics, drainage or dissolving; can appear weeks or months later
  • Granuloma or nodules that may require dissolving or, rarely, surgical management, and could be persistent
  • Filler embolism — filler entering a blood vessel and travelling elsewhere; very rare but can be serious
  • Damage to underlying structures (blood vessels, nerves, soft tissue)
  • Skin changes — pigment change, prolonged redness, scarring (including keloid)
  • Immediate hypersensitivity reaction, including, very rarely, anaphylaxis

Vascular occlusion & emergencies

A vascular occlusion occurs when filler blocks or compresses a blood vessel. Although rare, this is a medical emergency. Possible signs include:

  • Severe or increasing pain out of proportion to what's expected
  • Skin going white (blanching), or grey, blue, purple or mottled
  • Skin becoming cold, dusky or blistering
  • Signs of infection: spreading redness/heat, pus, fever, feeling unwell
  • Any breathing difficulty, chest pain, facial/throat swelling or feeling very unwell

I understand that if any of these occur I must contact Layali Clinic immediately on 07787 708200 or, if severe, call 999 / attend A&E. I understand HA filler can be dissolved with hyaluronidase, so acting quickly matters.

Longevity & breakdown

HA breast filler is naturally broken down (metabolised) by the body over time — typically around 12–18 months, though this varies. Factors such as high physical activity, fast metabolism, low body fat and lifestyle can affect how quickly it breaks down. Maintenance top-ups are usually needed to preserve the result.
